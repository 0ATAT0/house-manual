Serving the wet food Break it into chunks with a fork

It wants to be in chunks rather than a block, Luna especially. Pressing a fork into it does the job. A splash of water over the top helps with hydration.

If one of them isn't eating Normal for Luna, odd for Fred

Fred is a bit of a pig and will finish first. Luna eats much less and sometimes needs encouraging: hold a bit out on the fork to get her going.

Fred turning his nose up at a meal is the unusual one. If he skips two in a row, call us.

Saying hello Slow blinks, never a wide-eyed stare

Staring at them wide-eyed freaks them out after a while. A slow blink is how you tell them everything's fine.

Neither is much of a lap cat at the moment, so don't take it personally if they don't settle on you.

Sleep Out cold roughly 10am to 5pm

They sleep most of the day and will surface for lunch. Their beds are in our room, though they're rarely in them all night.

Play They tire each other out

The vet's line is fifteen minutes a day for a solo cat, and the two of them well exceed that between themselves. Mornings and evenings are their liveliest.

If they're still going when you want to sleep, just shut the bedroom door.

Litter tray Morning and evening, and it stinks

They usually go in the morning and again in the evening. Scoop it when you notice it.

There's no fixing the smell: every masking product is toxic to animals apart from candles. Open a window if the cats are shut safely in another room.
