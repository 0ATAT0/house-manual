Room by Room

Kitchen

Three machines, all straightforward.

Coffee The black machine makes pourover. Beans are in the black container next to the microwave; filter papers and the basket live in the cupboard below. Medium roast takes five minutes and is the everyday one. Cold brew takes ninety, so we set it going before bed.
Dishwasher Tablet in, press power on, then close the door. Closing it is what starts the cycle.
Washing machine Cotton setting. Press the temperature button once to drop it to 30, or leave it at 40. Tablets and fabric softener are on the shelf above.

Serving it

Wet food It wants to be in chunks rather than a block, Luna especially. Pressing a fork into it does the job. A splash of water over the top helps with hydration.
Dry food Straight in the bowl. The overnight portion goes down when you turn in.

If one isn't eating

Luna Normal. She eats much less than Fred and often needs a fork held out to get her going.
Fred Unusual, he's a pig. If he turns down two meals in a row, call us.

Living With Them

Saying hello

Staring at them wide-eyed freaks them out after a while. A slow blink is how you tell them everything's fine.

Neither is much of a lap cat at the moment, so don't take it personally.

Sleep and play

They sleep most of the day, roughly 10am to 5pm, surfacing for lunch. Their beds are in our room, though they're rarely in them all night.

They tire each other out, so there's nothing you need to do. If they're still going when you want to sleep, shut the bedroom door.

Litter tray

Morning and evening, usually. Luna buries hers, Fred makes no effort at all.

It stinks, and there's no fixing it: every masking product is toxic to animals apart from candles. Scoop when you notice, and open a window with the cats shut safely elsewhere.
